Education and qualifications

Biology first, then data science

Two degrees in the life sciences, a diploma that took me into data science, and the qualifications earned along the way.

How it shapes my teaching

  1. I learned the science before the code

    My first two degrees were in biology, not computer science. So I explain AI from the problem it solves, one idea at a time, and bring in code only when your goal needs it.

  2. Research taught me to test, not assume

    Lab work meant asking a clear question, running the experiment and checking the result. I build and teach AI the same way: build it, break it, rebuild it, and trust only what works.

  3. I've made the switch you might be making

    From life sciences to data science through a diploma and a six-month internship, then into a full-time data role. If you're coming to AI from another field, I can show you the route that worked.

  2. 2016 to 2018

    MSc Biotechnology and Bioinformatics

    Kerala University of Fisheries and Ocean Studies (KUFOS)

    Masters dissertation at IISER Thiruvananthapuram (Nov 2017 to Nov 2018) on periostin-integrin signaling in the regulation of hematopoiesis in the bone marrow niche, using cell culture, molecular biology and microscopy. The work led to a 2019 book chapter in Advances in Experimental Medicine and Biology.

    In plain words: bone marrow is where the body makes blood cells, and periostin is a protein in the marrow's supporting tissue. The question was how its signals, picked up by integrin receptors on the cells, help regulate that process.

    Bioinformatics sits where biology meets computing, the same crossing I made fully a few years later.
